# Event provisioning figures

Numbers, with the source that carries each one. Read this when you need a
figure; do not carry it in your head.

Everything here is drawn from **FEMA IS-15, *Special Events Contingency
Planning — Job Aids Manual*, March 2005** (a work of the US federal
government, public domain) unless another source is named. Page numbers are
the manual's own.

## How to use these, and how not to

These are **United States federal guidance**. Saudi Arabia applies the Saudi
Building Code, and the Civil Defence (الدفاع المدني) approves event sites.
So every figure here is a **defensible default, not the local requirement**.

When you use one, say where it came from and that it is a default:

> 30 female cubicles for 5 000 attendees (FEMA IS-15 p. 2-29, alcohol-free
> table) — a US federal default, to confirm with Civil Defence.

Never present a figure as a local legal requirement. If you need a number that
is not in this file, say you are assuming it rather than inventing a citation.

## Toilets

Assume a 50/50 male/female split unless the brief says otherwise. Saudi
Arabia is alcohol-free, so **the first table applies**; the second is here so
you can see the difference the assumption makes.

**Alcohol not available** (IS-15 p. 2-29)

| Attendees | Male WC | Urinals | Male sinks | Female WC | Female sinks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| < 500 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 6 | 2 |
| < 1 000 | 2 | 4 | 4 | 9 | 4 |
| < 2 000 | 4 | 8 | 6 | 12 | 6 |
| < 3 000 | 6 | 15 | 10 | 18 | 10 |
| < 5 000 | 8 | 25 | 17 | 30 | 17 |

**Alcohol available** (IS-15 p. 2-29) — for comparison only

| Attendees | Male WC | Urinals | Male sinks | Female WC | Female sinks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| < 500 | 3 | 8 | 2 | 13 | 2 |
| < 1 000 | 5 | 10 | 4 | 16 | 4 |
| < 2 000 | 9 | 15 | 7 | 18 | 7 |
| < 3 000 | 10 | 20 | 14 | 22 | 14 |
| < 5 000 | 12 | 30 | 20 | 40 | 20 |

The tables stop at 5 000. Above that, scale the 5 000 row linearly and say
that you did — the manual does not authorise it, and a plan that silently
extrapolates is a plan nobody can check.

**Reduce for shorter events** (IS-15 p. 2-29)

| Duration | Proportion of the table |
|---|---|
| more than 8 h | 100 % |
| 6–8 h | 80 % |
| 4–6 h | 75 % |
| under 4 h | 70 % |

**Also required**
- At least one unisex accessible toilet (p. 2-30).
- Separate toilets and hand-washing for food handlers (p. 2-30).
- Some units designated unisex, to shorten the female queue (p. 2-30).

**Where they go** (p. 2-28) — this is placement, not quantity
- Near hand-washing stations.
- **Away from food storage and food service areas.**
- Vehicle access for 24-hour servicing and pump-out.
- Lit, including the ground around them, if used after dark.
- Secured against tipping.

## Camping — a different table

For campgrounds over two to three nights (p. 2-32): male 1 WC per 50,
1 urinal per 100, 1 sink per 75, 1 shower per 100; female 1 WC per 25,
1 sink per 75, 1 shower per 100.

**Do not use these for a day event.** They are far more generous than the
event tables above, because people sleep there.

## Water

**20 litres of potable water per person per day**, of which 4.7 litres is
drinking water (IS-15 p. 2-28, given as 21 and 5 US quarts).

Consider duration, location and ambient temperature. On a hot arid coast this
is a floor, not a target. Drinking water must be reachable from the standing
areas, and the supply must be secured or supervised.

## Medical provision

(IS-15 p. 2-40)

| Attendees | Medical personnel | Medical posts |
|---|---|---|
| 500 | 2 | 1 |
| 1 000 | 4 | 1 |
| 2 000 | 6 | 1 |
| 5 000 | 8 | 2 |
| 10 000 | 12 | 2 |
| 20 000 | 22+ | 4 |

Every venue needs at least one climate-controlled facility with power and
running potable water. Below 500 attendees, close to hospital services,
dedicated medical provision is generally not required.

## Crowd density

Fruin's figures, as quoted in IS-15 (p. 2-9), converted to metres:

| Area per person | What happens |
|---|---|
| 2.30 m² | normal walking speed; can pass and avoid others |
| 0.93 m² | walking significantly restricted, speeds noticeably reduced |
| 0.46 m² | maximum capacity of a walkway — shuffling, movement only as a group |
| 0.28 m² | involuntary contact and brushing against others |
| **below 0.19 m²** | **dangerous crowd forces and psychological pressure develop** |

**Standing spectator ground with no seats: allow 0.37 to 0.46 m² per person
as a minimum** (IS-15 p. 2-11).

Read that carefully: 0.46 m² per person is the figure at which a walkway is
*at maximum capacity and shuffling*. It is a crush-adjacent number, not a
comfortable one. For a viewing area people should enjoy, budget far more —
around 1 m² per person — and treat 0.46 m² as the absolute floor at the
densest moment in front of a stage.

## Security

A family crowd needs about **1 officer per 1 000 spectators**; a more active
crowd, about **2 per 1 000** (IS-15 p. 2-35).

## Saudi-specific sources

Neither is summarised here — open them when the site or the brief needs them.

- **Saudi Building Code SBC 201** (2024), free from sbc.gov.sa — assembly
  occupancy classification and means of egress.
- **Universal Accessibility Guidelines**, King Salman Centre for Disability
  Research, free PDF — prescriptive dimensions, written for Saudi Arabia.
  Where these disagree with anything above, **these win**.

## Fire and access defaults, from US fire codes

Not from IS-15. These come from the International Fire Code chapter on tents
and membrane structures as adopted in US jurisdictions, and they are the
weakest-sourced figures in this file — sensible defaults to propose, nothing
more.

- Tents kept **6.1 m** from site boundaries, buildings, other tents, parked
  vehicles and combustion engines.
- Ground cleared of flammable material for **3 m** around a tent.
- Generators **6.1 m** from any tent or temporary structure.
- Cooking tents **3 m** apart from each other.
- Fire lane **6.1 m** wide, kept clear, with a turning area where a dead end
  runs past **46 m**.

An open generator running at 100 dB(A) needs **50 to 100 m** of separation to
stay under typical noise limits — a completely different constraint from the
6.1 m fire distance, and the one that decides whether a speech is audible.
